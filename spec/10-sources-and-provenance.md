# Sources and Provenance Registry

## Purpose

This registry separates evidence from design interpretation. A linked source may establish an object’s catalog data or discuss a geometric method; it does not automatically authorize image use, tracing, reproduction, or broad stylistic claims.

All links were checked on **2026-08-22**. Catalog records can change. Before production, re-check attribution, date, rights, image license, and current scholarship.

## Evidence levels

- **A — primary institutional record:** museum collection record, publisher record, or official standard.
- **B — peer-reviewed scholarship:** scholarly article or review; claims remain bounded by method and debate.
- **C — institutional interpretation:** museum essay or educational material synthesizing scholarship.
- **D — secondary orientation:** useful for discovery, not sufficient alone for a production claim.

Production motifs require at least one A-level object/source record plus relevant B/C interpretation. A source’s authority is domain-specific: a museum catalog supports its object record; a journal article supports its stated research claim.

## Core scholarly and institutional sources

### SRC-MET-001 — Geometric patterns overview

- **Title:** [Geometric Patterns in Islamic Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/geometric-patterns-in-islamic-art)
- **Publisher:** The Metropolitan Museum of Art
- **Level:** C
- **Supports:** Geometric ornament as a distinguishing but non-exclusive field; construction from circles, squares, stars, and multisided polygons; combination through repetition, interlace, and symmetry; coexistence with vegetal and calligraphic ornament; roots and exchanges extending to earlier traditions.
- **Use in this specification:** vocabulary, universal-core operations, and the requirement not to collapse geometric, vegetal, and calligraphic categories.
- **Does not support:** one universal symbolic meaning, a profile palette, direct motif copying, or the claim that all Islamic art is nonfigural.

### SRC-MET-002 — Characteristics of geometric decoration

- **Title:** [Primary Characteristics of Islamic Geometric Decoration](https://www.metmuseum.org/learn/educators/curriculum-resources/art-of-the-islamic-world/unit-three/primary-characteristics-of-islamic-geometric-decoration)
- **Publisher:** The Metropolitan Museum of Art
- **Level:** C
- **Supports:** regular tessellation as an underlying grid in many patterns; repetition and the visual suggestion of continuation beyond a frame; symmetry through repetition/mirroring; predominantly two-dimensional presentation with some interlocking/overlap effects.
- **Use in this specification:** grid families, bounded continuation, responsive cropping, and clear interlace.
- **Caution:** “Most” and contextual phrasing matter. These characteristics are not a rule for every place, period, object, or medium.

### SRC-MET-003 — Construction-oriented educational guide

- **Title:** [Islamic Art and Geometric Design: Activities for Learning](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/islamic_art_and_geometric_design.pdf)
- **Publisher:** The Metropolitan Museum of Art
- **Level:** C
- **Supports:** straightedge-and-compass educational constructions, underlying grids, repeat logic, and simple forms used to build complex arrangements.
- **Use in this specification:** progressive-construction diagrams and the distinction between construction grid and visible result.
- **Caution:** educational reconstructions are not proof that every historical workshop used the demonstrated sequence.

### SRC-SCI-001 — Girih and quasiperiodicity thesis

- **Citation:** Peter J. Lu and Paul J. Steinhardt, “[Decagonal and Quasi-Crystalline Tilings in Medieval Islamic Architecture](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1135491),” *Science* 315, no. 5815 (2007): 1106–1110. DOI: `10.1126/science.1135491`.
- **Level:** B
- **Supports:** the authors’ argument that a set of equilateral polygonal “girih tiles” can explain increasingly complex periodic patterns and that a fifteenth-century example approaches quasiperiodic/self-similar construction.
- **Use in this specification:** a researched example of polygonal tile construction and scale relationships.
- **Mandatory caveat:** This is an influential interpretation, not a license to label generic patterns “girih,” “Penrose,” or “quasicrystalline.”

### SRC-SCI-002 — Published critique

- **Citation:** Emil Makovicky, “[Comment on ‘Decagonal and Quasi-Crystalline Tilings in Medieval Islamic Architecture’](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1146262),” *Science* 318, no. 5855 (2007). DOI: `10.1126/science.1146262`.
- **Level:** B
- **Supports:** evidence that the quasiperiodicity interpretation is contested; the comment argues for a periodic reading of the Darb-i Imam pattern.
- **Use in this specification:** requires uncertainty language and prohibits presenting SRC-SCI-001 as scholarly consensus.

### SRC-SCI-003 — Authors’ response

- **Citation:** Peter J. Lu and Paul J. Steinhardt, “[Response to Comment on ‘Decagonal and Quasi-Crystalline Tilings in Medieval Islamic Architecture’](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1146352),” *Science* 318, no. 5855 (2007). DOI: `10.1126/science.1146352`.
- **Level:** B
- **Supports:** the original authors’ defense of a nonperiodic and self-similar reading for Darb-i Imam.
- **Use in this specification:** preserves both sides of the published exchange.

### SRC-NPJ-001 — Application-based review

- **Citation:** Mohammadreza Ranjazmay Azari, Mohammadreza Bemanian, Mohammadjavad Mahdavinejad, Axel Körner, and Jan Knippers, “[Application-based principles of Islamic geometric patterns; state-of-the-art, and future trends in computer science/technologies: a review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-022-00852-w),” *Heritage Science* 11, 2023. DOI: `10.1186/s40494-022-00852-w`.
- **Publisher platform:** Nature; journal: *Heritage Science*.
- **Level:** B
- **Supports:** a review of aesthetic-, structural-, and sustainability-oriented applications of Islamic geometric patterns and computational/digital research directions.
- **Use in this specification:** reinforces that contemporary use can involve structural and computational principles rather than formal surface imitation.
- **Caution:** A review taxonomy does not erase regional, temporal, material, or methodological differences among its sources. The journal is titled *Heritage Science*; “npj Heritage Science” is not the journal title and should not be used in formal citation.

## V&A collection orientation and object examples

### SRC-VA-000 — Collection overview

- **Title:** [Islamic Middle East — Explore the Collections](https://www.vam.ac.uk/collections/islamic-middle-east)
- **Institution:** Victoria and Albert Museum
- **Level:** C/A for links to V&A records
- **Supports:** orientation to the collection’s breadth across media, regions, and periods.
- **Use:** discovery only; profile decisions should cite individual object records below.

### SRC-VA-AND-001 — Umayyad Andalusi pyxis

- **Record:** [Pyxis, Córdoba/Madinat al-Zahra, 969–970](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O76682/pyxis-unknown/), museum no. 368-1880.
- **Level:** A
- **Material/context:** carved ivory casket with figural scenes and an Arabic inscription, made for Ziyad ibn Aflah.
- **Use:** demonstrates why “Andalusi” cannot be reduced to nonfigural geometry and why inscriptions, figural imagery, object function, and material must remain documented together.
- **Not an approved motif source:** the profile MUST NOT extract its inscription, figures, or carved ornament.

### SRC-VA-MAM-001 — Mamluk minbar

- **Record:** [Minbar made for Sultan Qa’itbay, probably Cairo, 1468–1496](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O48775/minbar-unknown/), museum no. 1050-1869.
- **Level:** A
- **Material/context:** cedar with joined, carved, turned, and fretted work; ivory/wood inlay; inscriptions; religious furnishing.
- **Use:** supports analysis of panel assembly, geometric joinery, and material hierarchy in the Mamluk profile.
- **Safeguard:** It is a mosque pulpit with inscriptions. No tracing, inscription use, sacred-object simulation, or context-free decorative extraction.

### SRC-VA-OTT-001 — Ottoman Iznik tile

- **Record:** [Tile from the Çinili Hamam, Istanbul, 1530s](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O205742/), V&A collection record.
- **Level:** A
- **Material/context:** blue-and-white fritware tile with `saz`-style vegetal composition, composite blossoms, rosettes, buds, stems, and leaves.
- **Use:** supports distinguishing Ottoman vegetal vocabulary from geometric pattern and documenting architectural provenance.
- **Safeguard:** Do not generalize one object into an all-period Ottoman palette or copy its composition.

### SRC-VA-PER-001 — Ardabil Carpet

- **Record:** [The Ardabil Carpet, Safavid Iran, 1539–1540](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O54307/the-ardabil-carpet-carpet-unknown/), museum no. 272-1893.
- **Level:** A
- **Material/context:** wool pile on silk foundation; central medallion, lamps, vegetal field, dated inscription; commissioned for the shrine of Shaykh Safi al-Din.
- **Use:** supports analysis of field/medallion/border hierarchy and dense vegetal precision in a specifically Safavid, textile, shrine-related context.
- **Safeguard:** No inscription use, shrine decontextualization, direct palette extraction, tracing, or use as a generic “Persian pattern.”

### SRC-VA-PER-002 — Iranian star-and-cross tile panel

- **Record:** [Tile Panel from the shrine of Imamzadeh Yahya, Varamin, 1262](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O89590/), multiple museum numbers listed in the record.
- **Level:** A
- **Material/context:** fifteen lustre-painted fritware star-and-cross tiles, probably Kashan; Qur’anic quotations surround the designs.
- **Use:** object-level evidence for star-and-cross module assembly and the inseparability of geometry, ceramic technique, shrine context, and epigraphy.
- **Safeguard:** Qur’anic inscriptions MUST NOT be extracted, imitated, obscured, or used decoratively. The panel is not a free pattern source.

### SRC-VA-MUG-001 — Mughal floral tile

- **Record:** [Glazed tile, Punjab, ca. 1650](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O34426/tile-tile-unknown/), V&A collection record.
- **Level:** A
- **Material/context:** floral composition with vase and arabesque detail, associated in the catalog record with the tomb of Madani in Srinagar and Shah Jahan-period refurbishment.
- **Use:** supports a carefully scoped Mughal profile distinction between flowering-plant/vase compositions and geometry.
- **Safeguard:** funerary association, uncertain production details, and collection history must remain visible; do not copy the panel or call all vase-and-flower designs Mughal.

## Rights and use

- A publicly accessible record is not a reproduction license.
- Record object-page terms and image-specific rights separately.
- Prefer independently constructed vector geometry over source-image reuse.
- Do not train or fine-tune a generator on collection images unless licenses, terms, and organizational policy explicitly permit it.
- Attribution requirements survive cropping and adaptation.
- Public-domain status does not waive cultural safeguards or the need to avoid sacred-text decoration.

## Claim-to-source rule

Each historical claim in a design rationale must cite the source that supports that exact claim. Sources cannot be cited decoratively. If a design decision is contemporary invention, label it as such rather than retrofitting historical authority.

Examples:

- Supported: “This educational diagram uses a square-grid construction informed by SRC-MET-003.”
- Supported with caution: “This research prototype explores the girih-tile interpretation argued in SRC-SCI-001; published debate is recorded in SRC-SCI-002 and SRC-SCI-003.”
- Unsupported: “Ten-point stars symbolize universal divine unity.”
- Unsupported: “Blue and gold are authentic Islamic colors.”

## Registry maintenance

- Re-check live links and catalog changes at least annually.
- Preserve accession identifiers when URLs change.
- Add source versions, archived citations where policy permits, and correction dates.
- Deprecate rather than delete a source used by released artifacts.
- Record disagreements and changed attributions.
- Profile review must include sources from scholars, institutions, and practitioners connected to the relevant regions; this initial registry is a baseline, not a complete or sufficiently representative canon.
