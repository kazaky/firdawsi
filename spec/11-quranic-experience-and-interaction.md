# Qur’an-guided experience and interaction

## Scope and non-claim

Firdawsi translates Qur’anic qualities associated with Jannah—peace, security, ease, nearness, abundance, beauty, and harmonious companionship—into contemporary interface principles. It **MUST NOT** claim to depict, reconstruct, visualize, or simulate Jannah. Revelation is the ethical and experiential source; garden architecture is a spatial interpretation; interface behavior is a documented design inference.

Qur’anic text and sacred calligraphy **MUST NOT** become decoration, texture, loading copy, achievement language, or interaction feedback. Translations used during research are interpretive aids and require citation. Any public theological claim requires qualified review.

## Source hierarchy

1. Qur’anic passage and reputable translation or tafsir context.
2. Explicit experiential quality inferred from the passage.
3. Interface law derived from that quality.
4. Token, component, or product behavior that implements the law.
5. Review record describing limits, accessibility, and cultural sign-off.

No implementation may jump directly from a verse fragment to visual styling.

## Primary experiential map

| Passage | Quality studied | Interface inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15:45–48 | security, welcome, freedom from rancor and fatigue | calm defaults, non-punitive recovery, no hostile or exhausting flows |
| 76:13–18 | shade, nearness, moderated conditions, flowing provision | reachable controls, comfortable density, content that comes near without chasing |
| 43:70–71 | welcome, delight, abundance | generous choice with clear hierarchy rather than scarcity pressure |
| 56:25–26 | peace in speech | humane, concise feedback; no shaming, alarmism, or manipulative urgency |
| 88:10–16 | order, readiness, elevated setting | composed surfaces, visible placement, purposeful spacing |
| 36:55–58 | joyful occupation, companionship, peace | flows support meaningful activity and settle into quiet completion |

These are design inferences, not translations or claims about the full meaning of the passages.

## Nine laws

1. **Peace over pressure.** Urgency is factual, never manufactured.
2. **Security over precarity.** Actions are reversible when possible and consequences are stated before commitment.
3. **Ease over friction.** The likely next action is near, reachable, and legible.
4. **Nearness over pursuit.** Context arrives where attention already is; users do not hunt through disconnected layers.
5. **Flow over interruption.** State changes preserve place and continuity.
6. **Abundance with order.** Rich options are progressively disclosed and grouped.
7. **Beauty in service.** Geometry frames hierarchy; it never competes with content.
8. **Companionship without coercion.** Collaborative and social patterns respect consent and privacy.
9. **Peaceful completion.** Success settles quietly and leaves the result visible.

## Interaction cycle

| Stage | Meaning | Observable behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sukūn** · rest | stable, readable default | no ambient motion required; action and consequence are visible |
| **Dunūw** · nearness | the control becomes easier to perceive and reach | hover, focus, touch target, or contextual placement clarifies availability |
| **Istijābah** · response | immediate acknowledgement | press, key, or voice input receives feedback within 120ms |
| **Jarāyān** · flow | the change proceeds without rupture | content rearranges in place; focus and reading position are preserved |
| **Istiqrār** · settling | the new state becomes stable | low-amplitude motion ends; updated content remains inspectable |
| **Salām** · peace | completion is clear without spectacle | concise confirmation, undo where relevant, no confetti or disruptive toast required |

Every interaction need not animate all six stages. It **MUST** preserve their causal order, work with keyboard and assistive technology, and remain understandable with motion disabled.

## Element behavior

- Primary commitment buttons MAY use the threshold arch; secondary, quiet, and destructive controls SHOULD use plain geometry unless a documented spatial threshold exists.
- Touch targets MUST be at least 44×44 CSS pixels. Touch acknowledgement MUST not depend on hover or precision.
- Navigation SHOULD preserve spatial context, expose the current place, and prefer stable landmarks over hidden gesture-only paths.
- Forms SHOULD validate near the field, preserve entered values, and move focus only when necessary.
- Destructive actions MUST name the object and consequence. Use undo for recoverable deletion; use confirmation for irreversible loss.
- Loading SHOULD keep the prior surface stable, describe what is happening, and avoid indefinite ceremonial motion.
- Success feedback SHOULD remain adjacent to the changed result long enough to be understood.

## Review record

Any feature claiming this foundation SHOULD record: source passage IDs, translation or tafsir consulted, experiential inference, interface law, implementation location, non-claim statement, accessibility result, cultural or scholarly reviewer, review date, and unresolved limitations.
